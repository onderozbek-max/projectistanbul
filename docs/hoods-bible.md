## 1. Rules for every hood

### 1.1 Fact policy

- Only facts the writer is confident in go in the postcard. Each has a confidence tag.
- **Street names are from memory.** Every street name is marked *(confirm)* and must be checked on a map before it goes in the game.
- If a hood has no fact I'm confident in, the field says **None yet** and gives a candidate to check.
- Dates and figures are given as ranges or decades when I'm not sure of the year.

### 1.2 Guardrails (narrative.md, *Writer Guardrails*)

- **Religious buildings:** background only. Never a target, a hazard, a boss location or a damage source. Never shown damaged.
- **Call to prayer (ezan):** ambient at most. Never a game cue, never a joke.
- **No flags, no state symbols, no police, no military, no *bekçi* (night watchman).** Kuzey Güvenlik is private and has no insignia.
- **No real brands and no transit livery.** The tram is generic red. Taxis are generic yellow. Shop names are fictional.
- **No real politics, no real people, no ethnic or regional jokes.** The joke is at the situation, or at Arda.
- **No child zombies.** Children do not appear as zombies.
- **Animals are never harmed.** Cats hiss, pigeons scatter, gulls fly. Fish is food.
- **Earthquake imagery banned.** Pavement pops and dust puffs only.

### 1.3 Authenticity checklist (per hood)

Each hood needs:
1. **One sound** a local would know (tram bell, simit call, ferry horn, dolmuş driver, ice-cream bell).
2. **One steam, smoke or light** that reads as food or weather (tea steam, grill smoke, bakery light). Smell is shown as visual, not as text.
3. **One person type** a local would recognise (tea seller, fisherman, bookseller, kapıcı).
4. **One landmark** (guardrail-safe).
5. **One detail that is fictional on purpose** (a shop name, a sign), so no real business is copied.

### 1.4 Arda's voice (for the hooks)

- **Deadpan.** He notices the absurd and says it flat.
- **Self-aware.** He knows he is in a zombie film. He does not play the hero.
- **Talks to zombies.** They groan back (the bark system). Keep lines short.
- **Visual gag first.** The joke must land without subtitles (product §3, pillar 1). The Turkish line is the spine; the English gloss must still be funny.
- **Never punches down** at a group, a religion, a region or a class.
- Hook types used below: *Deadpan*, *To zombies*, *Self-talk*, *Callback*, *Visual*.

### 1.5 Stage sub-locations (F)

Each hood has five sub-locations, one per stage. Each has a **time of day** and a **weather** that shift from stage to stage (for example, dusk and drizzle → night and fog). Route stages 1–4 and the boss arena (stage 5) all get their own space, so the walk never reuses one scene.

### H01 · İstiklal · Beyoğlu · km 1.0 · L1–5 · Boss: The Vatman
- **Feel:** a long pedestrian avenue. Shopfronts, bookshops, patisseries, and a nostalgic tram in the middle of the street.
- **Sound and people:** tram bell, simit calls, buskers, shop radios. Shoppers, students, waiters with trays, tourists with bags.
- **Steam and light:** chestnut and corn smoke puffing from carts. Warm shopfront light.
- **Landmarks:** the nostalgic tram line (generic red, no livery). The Tünel funicular station at the south end.
- **Streets (confirm):** İstiklal Caddesi; Tünel Meydanı.
- **Food:** kestane (chestnut), mısır (corn), simit, ocakbaşı grill, tulip-glass çay.
- **Zombie locals:** waiter (meyhane apron and tray, walker). Tourist (shopping bags, runner). Diner (turns after 2.5 s, W50).
- **Arda hook:** *Deadpan*. TR: "Vatman, ben de inmek istiyorum." EN: "Vatman, I'd like to get off too."

- **Postcard fact (high):** The Tünel funicular at İstiklal's south end opened in 1875.

1. **Topkapı and the Theodosian walls (narrative 121–130) are south-west of the northbound route.** The route moves the first Kuzgun ambush to the Mahmutbey depots (hood 25). The walls can stay as a Ch2 memory or a Ch3 reference. Owner decision.
