# NO SIGNAL: İSTANBUL — gamer audit, Bölüm 1–30

**16 November 2026 · Independent evaluation and implementation handoff · No gameplay changes**

## 1. Verdict

There is a game worth pursuing here: an ordinary young man trying to get home, Istanbul streets that become obstacles, improvised weapons, and friends whose jokes can turn into grief. Those are stronger reasons to care than another interchangeable zombie arena. The current build has also moved substantially beyond a generic survival timer: all 30 bölüms have authored route trials.

**It does not yet deliver the clarity, physical satisfaction, or dependable build payoff needed for the stated Survivor.io/Hades ambition.** The main problem is how the existing pieces combine. Combat, navigation, dialogue, tutorials, and optional services repeatedly compete for the same small area. A run can end successfully without delivering the weapon transformation its cards advertise. The relationship with Kaan promises a gameplay change that the current implementation has not embodied.

From the player’s seat, the opening currently reads as: “Interesting place and premise; let me see what I am doing. Why am I being told to stay here while the objective points elsewhere? Which upgrade will change my play? Why did my build reset just as it started getting interesting?” The improvement target is to replace those questions with: “I know the danger, I chose this build, that hit felt good, and I want to see what happens to these two.” These are evaluative interpretations, not quotes from recruited players.

**Recommendation: concentrate the next quality pass on L1–5, then prove Kaan’s arrival and the L25/L30 payoffs.** Do not expand the weapon catalogue or the campaign to solve these problems. Preserve the recent authored routes, Turkish dialogue revision, physical attack cues, death reactions, and service art; improve their delivery.

For comparison, use Survivor.io’s accessible one-hand combat and build combinations as the replay benchmark, and Hades as the aspirational standard for action and character integration. Official product references: [Survivor.io](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/survivor-io/id1528941310), [Hades](https://www.supergiantgames.com/games/hades/). This audit did not run a fresh side-by-side competitor test and cannot establish that one game is more compelling than either.

## 3. The big-picture quality gaps

| Dimension | What is already working | What prevents the target experience |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate hook | Distinct place, family motivation, absurd improvised arsenal | Cold open is more explanation than agency; the first physical exit is not obvious enough. |
| Combat mastery | Kiting, directional attacks, enemy windups, Dash, shield and ultimate exist | Hero and threats lose priority to text/portraits; success can feel like walking up a corridor rather than executing a recognizable tactical plan. |
| Build fantasy | Rank forks, weapon identities, passives, chests and evolutions exist | Broad offers, short runs and progression requirements can leave the strongest transformation unrealized. Advertised depth is not the same as delivered depth. |
| Encounter variety | Tram, carry, escort, wind, cranes, shutters and station tasks | Many different actions are introduced with the same ring/prompt grammar, in similarly framed corridors. Mechanical differences need stronger visible consequences. |
| Animation | Weapon-specific anticipation/contact/recovery, body deformation, reactions and finish effects | Physical acting at the hero’s actual display size is less convincing than the illustration quality of large portraits. More effects alone will not close that gap. |
| Story attachment | New TR lines are more situational; Mom, Kaan and the Captain have usable emotional material | Story frequently obscures the action. Kaan is much more present as a portrait than as a combat partner. |
| Next-run motivation | Fast next-level path, growth shortcut, unlocks, mastery and stars | Results emphasize accounting; the next concrete build experiment is not the dominant takeaway. The long 100-level journey can dwarf the immediate achievement. |

The three loops need to reinforce each other: **seconds** (read → move → hit → recover), **minutes** (choose → specialize → transform → resolve), and **across sessions** (earn something understandable → reconnect with a character → try a different plan). Right now, adding content to the last loop cannot compensate for interruptions in the first two.

## 4. Findings the implementation agent can act on

Priority means order of player impact, not production severity. Effort is relative: S = localized, M = several cooperating systems, L = art/system/content work. All proposed numbers and design changes below are experiments requiring the project’s normal owner/lead decision process; none are accepted canon or implemented by this audit.

### GA-01 · P0 · Give live combat one clear visual priority (M)

**Observed:** L1 Mom call, portrait bark, hold tutorial and route objective coexist. Later samples reproduce large portraits over approaching enemies and the objective marker: L17, L20, L23, L25, L27 and L28. L4’s streak announcement and L7’s prop hint also compete with speech. This persists despite the existing speech arbiter; do not add a second parallel arbiter.

**Player cost:** Reading becomes a defensive mistake; damage can feel arbitrary; the game teaches the player to ignore the writing it wants them to love.

**Proposed implementation:** Route all live overlays through the existing priority/layout mechanism, including phone calls, street invitations and streak celebrations. Reserve a safe area around Arda and imminent enemy warnings. Use compact live barks; stage essential long lines in a safe authored beat or a deliberate paused presentation. Queue expendable celebration text, and drop stale barks rather than playing them over the next action. Preserve localized text and current TR authorship.

**Where:** `prototype/js/view/speech.js`, `view/ui.js`, `view/street-invitations.js`, comic/call presentation and main event dispatch. Confirm the producers that bypass arbitration before changing ownership.

**Acceptance:** At narrow phone layouts, recorded L1 call + enemy windup + service hint + streak and L23 sack interaction + Kaan bark show no portrait/text over Arda, the relevant threat footprint, or the required marker. Only one actionable instruction leads. Repeat with largest supported text and reduced motion; important dialogue remains available. A new player can point to the immediate threat and destination without closing a panel.

Evidence: [L1 live call](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/l1-call-and-objective.jpg), [L23 competing layers](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/l23.jpg), [L28 conflicting instruction](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/l28.jpg).

### GA-02 · P0 · Make navigation and the win transition trustworthy (S–M)

**Observed:** In L1 I initially pushed against the restaurant frontage before finding its left-side opening. “HALKANIN İÇİNDE KAL” persisted while the required route was elsewhere and after the boss died. The final side exit was easy to overshoot; Arda could enter the top HUD area. During the victory transition the route instruction returned to defeating the already-defeated Lokantacı. Source confirms `main.js:onWin` closes the L1 shutter for the cinematic while the ordinary route display remains active. This was a misleading display, not a softlock.

**Player cost:** The player cannot tell whether an instruction is stale, optional, or mandatory. Getting lost after winning steals the payoff.

**Proposed implementation:** Highlight the actual passable doorway and final side exit, including an approach cue. Scope hold prompts to the specific active interaction and clear them when it ends or becomes irrelevant. Anchor optional-service hints to their own object with explicit optional status. Keep the hero/exit inside the usable viewport at route ends. Once settlement starts, replace the route HUD with a success/cinematic state. Use the localized DEPAR term consistently; the first hint currently says DASH.

**Where:** `sim/director.js`, `view/ui.js`, objective/hint renderers, camera/end bounds, `main.js:onWin` and `updateExit`. Do not change collision or win conditions merely to hide a display problem.

**Acceptance:** Fresh L1: doorway found without instruction from the tester; no obsolete hold prompt during travel/boss/exit; crossing the exit never reintroduces a boss requirement; hero and next interaction remain visible. Add a focused state-transition assertion for the stale prompt/win HUD and inspect the actual phone layout.

Evidence: [L1 finale hierarchy](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/l1-finale-hierarchy.jpg).

### GA-03 · P0 · Deliver a deliberate build before asking for another run (M)

**Observed/data:** L1 starts with seven eligible base weapons and eight passives; the catalogue expands later. Cards advertise evolution requirements, but a normal short route provides limited time and choices. The default weapon-first fresh campaign produced **0 evolutions in 30 clears**, despite **29 runs with rank forks** and four ending with a rank-5 base weapon. A knife/gloves-focused probe produced **2 evolutions in 29 attempts**, at L20 and L25, and 26 runs with forks. Those two evolutions had roughly 39 and 64 seconds before the run ended. Existing forks therefore work; “there is no build progression” would be false.

The default policy prefers weapons over the prerequisite passive, and the focused policy does not strategically reroll. These are stress cases showing how readily a player can miss the advertised transformation, not measured human odds.

**Player cost:** “+36% damage” is legible arithmetic but a weak promise of a new playstyle. Resetting a broad, unfinished collection gives less reason to replay than testing an intentional build.

**Proposed implementation:** First instrument offers, chosen direction, prerequisite acquisition, fork time, evolution eligibility, chest timing and usable post-transformation combat. Then prototype a narrower early choice pool and visible build goals. Show an immediate behavioral description beside damage: what it hits, where, and why the player might want it. Show the missing prerequisite directly. Prefer strengthening existing rank-3 forks and ensuring a reachable evolution path over adding more weapons or lengthening every run. Keep several viable choices; do not silently script every run into the same build.

**Where:** `config-weapons*.js`, `sim/progression.js`, chest offer logic, card UI, telemetry. Changes to pool/rates/numbers belong in configuration and the review’s Deviations.

**Proposed experiment gate:** By the end of L3–5, an uncoached tester can intentionally reach one visibly different build and name its tradeoff. In a seeded policy matrix, seeking a shown recipe should reliably succeed while leaving at least one meaningful encounter to use it. Log “eligible but no chest,” “missing passive,” and “rank too low” separately. Set a numerical success target after baseline human observation; do not mistake this audit’s 2/29 for a tuning target.

Evidence: [card screen](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/card-choice.jpg), [late card screen](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/l29.jpg), [raw choice/event probe](telemetry/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/build-probe.jsonl).

### GA-04 · P1 · Investigate the L13–14 attrition spike before balancing globally (M)

**Data:** L13 cleared 6/8, with only 3/8 death-free. L14 cleared 5/8, with only 2/8 death-free. Six of L14’s nine recorded deaths were attributed to bloaters. L19 cleared 8/8 but only 3/8 death-free; L5 was 8/8 and 4/8 respectively. Recovery hides the friction when reporting only clear rate.

**Player cost:** A surprise contact/burst chain feels different from a readable mistake. A revive can conceal a frustrating encounter without making it satisfying.

**Proposed implementation:** Reproduce L14 seeds with bloater deaths using expected earned power and actual Dash/shield use. Inspect warning visibility, obstacle escape width, overlapping damage sources, objective commitment and recovery intervals. Adjust the specific interaction only after identifying the cause. Keep the readable threat and punishable mistake; do not simply reduce all damage or boost health. L13/14 should not accidentally punish the lesson taught by the required hold/carry task.

**Where:** `sim/karakoy-trial.js`, `sim/route-milestones.js`, enemy behavior/damage, relevant config and enemy telegraph rendering.

**Acceptance:** Every reproduced death has an identifiable warning and reachable response. Compare the same seed set before/after with recovery disabled and enabled, plus a policy using active skills. No new route stalls. Human testers can explain the mistake and demonstrate a better second attempt. Eight seeds identify a hotspot, not a statistically stable win-rate target.

### GA-05 · P1 · Establish the animation bar with three complete attack performances (L)

**Observed/source:** The large illustrated Arda has more personality than the small combat actor. The current code already has weapon-specific windups, contact cues, recovery, hand grips, body deformation, crowd reactions and death finishes. `view/hero-body.js` separates the illustrated body around waist/ankles; `hero-attacks.js` handles competing poses. These are useful foundations, not missing systems to rebuild by assumption.

**Player cost:** When the effect carries most of the attack, the weapon can feel like something attached to Arda rather than something he is exerting force through. A crowded six-weapon build further obscures ownership of the hit.

**Proposed implementation:** Produce a reviewed knife, sledgehammer and thrown-canister sequence at actual phone scale: anticipation, planted weight transfer, contact/release, recovery, travel/landing where applicable, and enemy reaction. Demonstrate rank 1 and a transformation. Keep contact synchronized to the sim event and preserve responsive movement. Test mixed attacks before extending the treatment to every weapon. Use the existing Weapon Lab as the comparison fixture. New rigs/pipelines require a proposal; this audit is not authorization to restart Godot or add a dependency.

**Acceptance:** In normal-speed phone captures, observers identify the three attack types without labels and identify which contact caused the reaction. Hammer weight reads through body motion, knife stays quick, throw visibly leaves the hand. Mixed builds preserve boss warnings. Validate reduced motion and the existing no-flash guardrail. Capture frame timings on target hardware; static screenshots cannot certify Hades-quality motion.

### GA-06 · P1 · Make Kaan joining change play, then pay it off at L30 (L)

**Source-backed gap:** `config-bond.js` explicitly states that Kaan has no body in the sim. It joins his bond after L20 and awards bonuses; only Arda is listed in the save’s hero roster. The PRD slice specifies Kaan joining and tag-switch in L30 phase 3, with the join timing itself marked proposed. This is an existing slice-intent gap, not a request for a new party beyond the slice.

**Player cost:** “We are together now” is less convincing if the companion only supplies dialogue and a percentage. The L25 Captain scene asks for attachment before the player has done much with him.

**Proposed implementation:** Resolve the approved slice scope first, then give L21–24 a visible, readable cooperative action that helps in the route or combat and expresses Kaan’s protective role. Make the player understand when and why he acts. Use the intended L30 tag-switch payoff if retained by the lead. Avoid turning the companion into an escort liability or adding another mandatory button without phone testing.

**Where:** `config-bond.js`, `meta/meta-save.js`, companion simulation/view, `sim/bridge-trial.js`, `sim/eminonu-trial.js`, Sirkeci finale and dialogue event conditions.

**Acceptance:** After joining, a player can identify something Kaan did mechanically and why they value him. L25’s aftermath follows an action they experienced together. L30’s intended switch/team payoff is playable and comprehensible, or the owner explicitly revises that scope. Preserve save compatibility and sim/view separation.

### GA-07 · P1 · Make each district’s mechanics visible in the world (M–L)

**Observed/source:** There are real differences—escorts, wind screens, carried items, controls, rail movement—yet the first read often remains “walk up a similarly wide corridor to another hold ring.” Galata’s repeated cobblestone pattern is visually busy; the small tower reads more like a placed object than a dominant landmark. The bridge and station introductions promise stronger spatial identities than the repeated central corridor framing delivers.

**Proposed implementation:** Give each district one dominant tactical verb and a visible consequence, using the existing trials: tram timing; slope/escort positioning; quay controls that rearrange access; bridge cover against wind; moving cargo through spice hazards; station routing and signals. Stage an easy introduction, a pressured application and a finale combination. Show the thing moving/opening, not only a progress circle completing. Quiet floor contrast and scale landmarks consistently before adding decoration.

**Where:** district trial modules, corresponding milestone FX/layout config and environment view. Keep passability and collision aligned with illustrations.

**Acceptance:** A player shown unlabeled gameplay can distinguish the six districts by both spatial cue and action. They can explain how completing the objective changed the street. Do not require six entirely new systems; demonstrate this first in one existing Galata and one bridge route.

### GA-08 · P1 · Stage the story around action and give emotional moments room (M)

**Observed/source:** The current native TR rewrite contains specific, grounded material. The Captain recalls giving Kaan an apron and asking him to return; that is stronger than adding another generic joke. Mom’s food questions humanize the emergency. But large mid-combat portraits make the player choose between reading and surviving, while reusable bust compositions flatten the difference between routine banter and a major loss.

**Proposed implementation:** Preserve the current draft’s voice. Separate essential plot, useful tactical speech and optional banter in timing/priority. Trigger a remark when its subject is visible or its consequence happens. Give L20 reunion, L25 aftermath and L30 handoff distinct staging, expressions, silence and reaction. Protect the Captain aftermath from celebratory quips. On retry, shorten previously seen setup and acknowledge the retry where appropriate without pretending the canonical story advanced. A dedicated scene composition is more valuable here than another interchangeable portrait.

**Where:** `js/dialogue/`, story/event selection, comic layout/art and view speech. TR and EN remain independently written; do not translate the TR revision into EN as part of a UI fix.

**Acceptance:** Uncoached players can explain why Arda is traveling, what changed with Kaan and why the Captain matters. They do not need to stop moving under attack to learn essential information. Repeat attempts do not replay a long mandatory setup. Test sound-on and muted; the audible performance still needs a separate pass.

### GA-09 · P2 · End each run with one concrete reason to start the next (M)

**Observed:** L1 results provide useful stats and a next button, and growth is reachable. However, metrics and reset explanations dominate the first view; the newly available item is less prominent. The map’s small progress against 17 km/100 levels makes a completed run feel tiny. The 100 count is the intended journey, not a bug.

**Proposed implementation:** Lead results with what the player accomplished, the consequential unlock/upgrade, and one next experiment. Examples of function, not final localized copy: try the newly unlocked rebound weapon in the next encounter; choose a fork that counters a threat you just met. Show the local five-level district milestone alongside the longer journey. Explain which power persists and which build resets through the result itself. Do not add another currency or reward screen.

**Where:** results/map/growth UI, reward presentation and localization keys.

**Acceptance:** After L1 and L5, an uncoached tester can name what they kept, what resets, and one thing they want to try. They can spend an available upgrade and start the next bölüm without coaching. Measure voluntary next-run choice, not only button clicks during a required session.

Evidence: [L1 results](media/R1/gamer-audit-2026-11-16/l1-results.jpg).

### GA-10 · P1 parallel gate · Verify phone feel and restore trustworthy checks (M)

**Gap:** This audit cannot settle one-thumb feel, audio mix or sustained iOS performance. Existing desktop assertions and earlier reports are useful but insufficient. The full suite is currently not green.

**Work:** Test a named supported iPhone for a complete L1–5 session, crowded mixed build, pause/background/resume, card selection while moving, Dash/skill reach, left-handed layout and readable text. Record device/OS/settings, frame-time percentiles, input issues and audio observations. The PRD calls for Low p95 ≤36 ms at 300 enemies, Mid/High p95 ≤18 ms; use the approved device matrix rather than changing the target to match a desktop result. Verify the physical action still reads with reduced effects and muted audio.

**Checks to resolve:** Four suites failed to bind localhost in this sandbox (`arena-evolve`, `art-raster-world`, `art-raster`, `map-first-run`); rerun in an environment permitting their local server. `enemy-silhouettes` failed its headless Chrome mask render; investigate environment versus asset failure. `fonts` failed one assertion on `street-invitations.js` naming `CM Kalam` before system fallbacks; inspect the accepted font-family rule rather than assuming the rendered font is wrong. `modes` failed Normal reference traces L12-s9 and L30-s4; reconcile intended behavior with traces before updating any reference.

**Acceptance:** Required suites pass with reasons recorded for any reference change, and real-device evidence meets the applicable target. Do not label the build merge-ready from this audit. No baselines or production code were changed here.

## 6. Recommended implementation sequence

1. **Readable L1:** GA-01 + GA-02. Preserve current mechanics, repair instruction lifetimes, exits and overlay priority. Deliver a full ordinary-speed L1 phone capture with no coaching. This can be scoped as the first implementation task.
2. **A build worth repeating:** GA-03, initially L1–5. Instrument the current progression, then compare a focused onboarding/recipe experiment against the baseline. Preserve distinct viable builds and record numerical deviations.
3. **Fair pressure:** GA-04 on L5, L13–14 and L19, using both no-revive results and actual active-skill play. Do not use raw bot clear rate as the sole balance target.
4. **Animation proof:** GA-05 with knife/hammer/throw; GA-07 with one Galata and one bridge encounter. Approve actual phone motion before multiplying assets across the catalogue.
5. **Relationship payoff:** GA-06 + GA-08 across L20–30. Resolve companion scope, implement the accepted mechanical payoff, and stage reunion/Captain/finale around it.
6. **Reason to return:** GA-09, then an uncoached campaign test. GA-10 runs alongside the sequence and gates claims about phone quality or merge readiness.

The first implementation chat should take **GA-01/02 as a bounded task**, read the existing speech-arbiter and route-state code, reproduce the supplied scenes, and return before/after evidence. It should not treat this entire document as one giant branch or replace the current Turkish draft. Record accepted design changes in the project decision log and numerical changes under Deviations, following the existing workflow. No production design or architecture decision has been changed by this report.

## 7. How to know the game is improving

Start with five to eight uncoached target players on supported iPhones. Include survivor-like players and people who care about action/story games; record prior familiarity. This is a diagnostic round, not enough to estimate commercial retention. Let them stop naturally; do not require them to finish 30 levels and then interpret compliance as engagement.

| Test | Observation to collect | Proposed diagnostic pass |
|---|---|---|
| Opening without explanation | Time to first movement, first intentional hit/pick, doorway hesitation, ignored overlays | Most testers navigate and describe the immediate objective without help. |
| Three card choices | Ask afterward why they picked, what they expected, and what changed | Player predicts a behavioral difference and recognizes it during play. |
| First serious failure | Ask what caused it before displaying extra coaching; observe retry | Player identifies a readable cause and attempts a different response. |
| End of L5 | Ask what persisted and what they want to try next; offer a natural stopping point | Voluntary continuation/retry has a concrete build or story reason. |
| L13–14 targeted revisit | Record approach, warning visibility, damage sequence and recoveries | Better play produces an understandable improvement, not dependence on a revive. |
| L20–25 | Ask what Kaan did and why the Captain matters | Mechanical cooperation and emotional recall support each other. |
| L30 | Ask what changed, what they learned, and what they expect next | A satisfying slice resolution plus a specific next curiosity. |
| Next day | Observe voluntary return and chosen activity without repeated reminders | Separate genuine return from a scheduled mandatory test session. |

Then use a larger comparison round to test the PRD’s own bars: at least 70% voluntarily past L10, “would keep playing” at least 4/5, at least 60% citing setting/story, and at least 50% blind preference on animation/game feel against a genre leader. Keep identical devices and comparable viewing/play conditions; separate visual preference from hands-on control. Do not call a preference result Hades parity or promise retention from these simulation numbers.

Record choice-to-payoff time, time spent searching for an objective, unread/overlapping essential dialogue, first-attempt deaths, revive usage, chosen build diversity, and voluntary replays. These explain why a player continues or quits more usefully than aggregate kills or a green completion trace alone. Audio should get its own audible assessment: can the player distinguish enemy warning, weapon contact, pickup and dialogue on phone speakers and with music playing?
