# Style bible (upload this to the ChatGPT Project as a file)

Long form of the Project instructions. Where the two differ, this file wins.

## 1. What the pictures are used for
- The game is drawn on a phone canvas about 390 x 844 points. A normal enemy is about 50 points tall (about 150 device pixels), a boss about 140 points (about 420 device pixels).
- Dozens of figures overlap. The player must always be findable, enemy warnings must always be visible, and the ground must stay a mid-value grey so zombies, green XP drops and red warnings pop.
- So: strong outer silhouettes, flat colour blocks, one bold outline, no fine detail that disappears at 50 pixels.

## 2. Camera and pose
- Top-down three-quarter, about 55 degrees above the ground. The top of the head is visible, the face is visible, the feet are visible.
- Figures face right (a profile-leaning three-quarter, near shoulder to the viewer). The game mirrors for left.
- Feet sit on one baseline at the bottom centre of the picture. The figure fills about 80% of the picture height. Leave an even margin all round.
- A figure is about three heads tall: a big head, a short torso, short thick limbs, large hands and feet. The same proportions for every human and zombie, so they look like one world.

## 3. Line, shading, light
- One outline weight for every figure: near-black #14151F, about 3% of the figure height, round line ends, closed shapes.
- Inside the outline: flat fills. Each colour has a base tone, a shadow tone (darker, slightly cooler) and at most one highlight tone (lighter, slightly warmer).
- Light comes from the upper left (warm amber). Shadow tones sit on the lower right of each form (cool, a touch of bosphorus blue #1B4965).
- No gradients, no airbrush, no noise or fabric texture. At most a few short flat hatch lines for torn cloth.

## 5. The hero, Arda (the reference for everything human)
A 20-year-old Turkish university student. Wiry, medium height. Short dark wavy hair swept up into a quiff. Light stubble. Expressive eyebrows. Red hoodie #D7263D with the hood down and a grey t-shirt collar showing. Dark navy jeans. White sneakers with a grey sole. Earbuds with the cable hanging round his neck. Hands bare. Empty hands unless a request says otherwise.

## 6. Zombies (all of them)
Desaturated olive skin #7D8B6A family, pale glowing eyes (a flat pale yellow-green #E4F0A8 with a black pupil, unless a request names another eye colour: the runner, the brute and the screamer have red-orange eyes), open mouths, torn everyday clothing. No gore. Each enemy TYPE has its own silhouette feature and its own accent colour, given in its request. Zombies share the hero's three-heads-tall proportions.

## 7. Sheets and frames
- A sheet is one image with several poses in an even grid with wide empty margins. Same scale, same baseline, nothing touching the cell edge, no labels.
- Walk frames: walk1 and walk2 are the two contact poses of a walk cycle (left foot forward and right foot forward). They are used alternately, so they must be mirror-complementary and the body must sit at the same height.
- Hit frame: the figure flinches back, head tilted, one arm raised. Dead frame: collapsed flat on the ground, seen from above, at the same line weight.

## 8. Things that must never appear
Text of any kind (including shop signs and labels on objects), numbers, logos, real brands, flags, religious symbols, gore, blood pools, exposed organs, photorealism, watermarks, signatures, frames and borders, cast shadows on the ground, glows around figures, any background other than the flat colour named in the request.

## 9. Ground tiles and props
- Ground tiles are full pictures, seen straight down from above (not three-quarter), perfectly seamless on all four edges, mid-value grey (stone grey #8A8F98 range), no objects, no shadows, no lighting gradient across the tile, joints in night ink.
- Props are drawn three-quarter top-down like characters, on the flat background colour, with the same outline weight, and a flat shadow-tone on the lower right only.

## 10. Quality bar
Compare with the attached current-look references: the new art must keep every silhouette feature and accent colour of the current figures, but look hand-drawn and finished: confident line, deliberate shapes, pleasing colour. If an image looks airbrushed, photographic, painterly, 3D-rendered or noisy, it is wrong.
